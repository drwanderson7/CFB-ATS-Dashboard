Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 26, 2026

PickGauge is a small, independently-run tool for tracking against-the-spread picks in college football pick'em pools. This page explains what information the app collects, how it's used, and what control you have over it.

Account information

Signing in is handled entirely by Clerk, a third-party authentication provider. PickGauge never sees or stores your password — Clerk manages that directly. The only account information PickGauge itself uses is your Clerk user ID (an internal account identifier, not your password) and, where you've provided one, your email address for sign-in purposes. See Clerk's own privacy policy for how they handle sign-in data.

What PickGauge stores about your use of the tool

Once you're signed in, the following is saved to a private database bucket tied to your account, and is not visible to any other user:

  • Your picks, pool entries, and which pools you've joined
  • Pool sheets you've imported (game lines, pick limits, entry names)
  • Prediction-input data you've imported or entered (e.g. from a Powers PDF), and which prediction systems and weights you've enabled

Separately, a small amount of data is shared across every signed-in user of this deployment — current game lines, prediction-system averages, and the structure of any pool someone has published (games, pick limits). Your individual picks and entries are never included in that shared data — only the pool's structure is shared, so others can join and pick within it.

Your Odds API key stays on your device — never persisted on our end. If you add a personal key from The Odds API in Settings, it's stored only in your browser's local storage. When you hit Refresh Lines, it's transmitted securely (HTTPS) to PickGauge's own serverless proxy in a request header, so the proxy can call The Odds API on your behalf — it is never written to our database, never synced to your account, and never included in a backup file you export.

Other services this tool talks to

PickGauge is built on a few third-party services, each governed by its own privacy policy:

  • Clerk — authentication and sign-in
  • Vercel — hosting for the app and its backend functions
  • Upstash — the database that stores your synced picks/pools/inputs
  • The Odds API and thepredictiontracker.com — sources for game lines and prediction-system data (no personal information is sent to either)

Exporting and deleting your data

Export: Settings → Export backup (.json) downloads everything synced to your account as a plain JSON file, anytime.

Deletion: Settings → Delete my PickGauge data permanently deletes everything in your synced PickGauge account state — every pool, pick, entry, and archived week — on every signed-in device. This is separate from your sign-in itself (email/password/session), which you manage from your account menu. Aggregate product analytics are not part of that synced account-state record: PickGauge does not keep a user-level analytics event history that can be exported or replayed, and aggregate counters age out after about 400 days. Feedback you intentionally submit through the beta form is stored separately and also ages out after about 400 days. If you'd like submitted feedback removed sooner, or would rather not use the in-app deletion button for your synced state, contact us.

Product analytics and beta feedback

PickGauge uses a small first-party analytics system to understand whether core features are being used and where beta users run into friction. No Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, advertising tracker, or other third-party analytics script is loaded.

The analytics system stores daily aggregate counts for a short allowlist of product actions (for example opening the app, changing tabs, refreshing lines, importing a pool, or exporting a Snapshot) plus coarse context such as mobile/desktop and Overall/pool view. It does not store a raw clickstream, your picks, model numbers, imported file contents, or your email address. A one-way hashed account identifier is used only to estimate unique users; analytics data expires after about 400 days.

If you intentionally submit the in-app beta feedback form, PickGauge stores the feedback text, category, time, current app section, mobile/desktop context, and a pseudonymous account identifier so reports can be grouped. The form does not automatically attach screenshots, picks, model numbers, or imported-file contents. Beta feedback also expires after about 400 days. If you need a reply, use the support email shown in the app rather than the feedback form.

Cookies

PickGauge's first-party analytics does not set an analytics cookie. Clerk sets its own session cookies to keep you signed in; see their policy for details.
